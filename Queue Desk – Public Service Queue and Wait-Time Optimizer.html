    How the estimate works

    Each estimate replays the line against the open counters: every counter has a time when it frees up (remaining time for the current customer, using the average service time learned from completed tokens). Waiting tokens are assigned in order, priority first, to whichever counter frees up first. A queueing-theory check (Erlang C, M/M/c) compares this to the predicted average wait and suggests how many counters to staff, using a short-term forecast of arrivals.

    Designing for the person in the queue

    Queue Desk is built around human-computer interaction (HCI) principles: the people using it are stressed, often in a hurry, and range from first-time smartphone users to experienced counter staff. The interface is meant to be understood at a glance, usable without training, and accessible to people with different abilities and languages.

    Who it is for

    Rukhsana, 68, retired teacher

    Visits the hospital outpatient desk. Uses a basic smartphone, prefers Urdu, and finds small text hard to read.

    Design response: QR code so she never types a web address, Urdu layout, text size up to 145%, voice read-aloud, a priority lane, and one big button per screen.

    Bilal, 24, university student

    Renewing an ID card between classes. Wants to leave the building and come back at the right time.

    Design response: honest wait estimate, alerts when a set number of people are ahead, a warning when the estimate changes by 10 minutes or more, and tracking by token number from any phone.

    Sana, 35, counter operator

    Handles a constant flow of people and has no time to read manuals. Needs to trust the numbers.

    Design response: one dashboard for demand, counters and queue, a staffing suggestion with the reason, a what-if preview, and a log of what changed and its effect.

    Usability heuristics applied

    Heuristic (Nielsen)How Queue Desk applies it
    Visibility of system statusA four-step progress tracker, live place in line and estimated wait, the shared "Now serving" board, and screen-reader announcements when status changes.
    Match with the real worldA paper-ticket look, plain wording such as "people ahead", and waits shown in minutes rather than queueing-theory terms. Full Urdu layout, right to left.
    User control and freedomNo account needed. Staff can cancel a counter closing with "Keep open", switch to manual mode, or pause the simulation. Citizens can take a new token after a skip.
    Consistency and standardsStatus always uses the same words, icon and colour across the token page, tracker and board. "Token" and "counter" mean the same thing everywhere.
    Error preventionButtons are disabled when an action is not possible (call next with an empty queue, apply a suggestion that matches current staffing). Token search forgives case, spaces and missing zeros.
    Recognition rather than recallQuick-pick token chips, a QR code instead of a typed address, and the ticket always shows what you need to know.
    Flexibility and efficiencyWalk-up QR for new users, the Enter key to track, and a one-click staffing suggestion for experienced staff.
    Minimalist designOne primary action per screen. Staff tools sit on a separate screen so citizens never see them.
    Recover from errorsIf a token is not found, the message says what to check on the ticket. A skipped token explains why and offers a new one.
    Help and documentationShort explanations next to controls, and a "How the estimate works" panel for staff and judges.

    Accessibility features

    • Text size up to 145% and a high-contrast mode, saved for the next visit.
    • Status never relies on colour alone: every state has an icon and a word.
    • Everything works from the keyboard, with a visible focus ring and a skip link.
    • Screen-reader announcements for status changes, and labelled controls.
    • Voice alerts and "Read status aloud" using the browser's speech engine. Voice availability varies by device, and an Urdu voice may not be installed.
    • Touch targets of at least 44 px, and animation is switched off for people who prefer reduced motion.
    • Urdu and English for the citizen screens, with right-to-left layout.
    • A voice assistant that registers a token by conversation in Urdu or English, with a typing box as a fallback for people who cannot or would rather not speak.

    The design aims at WCAG 2.2 AA. It has not had a formal audit, so treat this as a design intent to verify with testing.

    Usability test plan

    • Participants: 5 to 8 people, including at least two older adults and two non-English readers.
    • Task 1: get a token and say how long you expect to wait.
    • Task 2: close the app, then find where your token is using only the number on the ticket.
    • Task 3: respond to the "turn approaching" alert.
    • Task 4 (staff): bring the wait under target during a rush.
    • Measure: task completion, time on task, errors, a 1 to 5 ease rating, and the System Usability Scale (SUS) questionnaire.
